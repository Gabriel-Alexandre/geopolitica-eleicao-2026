President Trump on Thursday signed an
executive order
removing tariffs on Brazilian beef, coffee and other goods that were previously subject to a 40% levy which Mr. Trump announced in July.
Mr. Trump cited "various officials" whom he said advised him that "certain agricultural imports from Brazil should no longer be subject" to the 40% tariff, in part because of progress the U.S. has made in its trade negotiations with Brazil.
The order applies to Brazilian imports to the U.S. on or after Nov. 13, according to the order. Refunds could be owed to businesses that paid the duties, Mr. Trump noted.
Mr. Trump last week
cut tariffs on beef, coffee, bananas
and other agricultural imports as Americans grapple with high prices at the grocery store.
That tariff relief came a day after Mr. Trump
announced reciprocal trade agreements
with Argentina, Guatemala, El Salvador and Ecuador. At the time, senior administration officials said the agreement frameworks were largely focused on allowing those foreign markets to accept more U.S. goods.
Food prices
rose 3.1%
in September compared to one year ago, while roasted coffee had risen nearly 19% over the same period, according to federal data.
