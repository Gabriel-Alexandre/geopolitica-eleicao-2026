Trump’s Attempt to Punish Cassidy Hutchinson Ends in a Flop
A grand jury failed to indict the former White House aide.
Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc/Getty Images
The Trump administration’s attempt to prosecute former White House aide Cassidy Hutchinson over her testimony to the House January 6 committee has hit a snag.
A federal grand jury in Washington, D.C., declined to indict Hutchinson,
The Washington Post
reported
Tuesday evening, citing two anonymous sources. Neville S. Hedley, who told U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui earlier that day that he was acting senior litigation counsel at the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division, had warned in court that the jury could not agree on an indictment. Hedley didn’t mention a name, but the Post’s sources said it was Hutchinson.
President Donald Trump and his Republican allies have been seeking charges against Hutchinson for
months
, accusing her of lying to the investigative committee. In March, Representative Barry Loudermilk referred her to the Department of Justice, and House Judiciary Committee Chair Jim Jordan signed off on it.
Hutchinson was one of the star witnesses in the committee’s hearings, claiming that Trump was very agitated on January 6, 2021, and fully aware of the potential for violence. She
testified
that he wanted to march with his supporters on the Capitol, and was furious when his aides and Secret Service detail said that it wouldn’t be safe.
Trump even tried to grab the steering wheel of the presidential limousine known as The Beast, and when a Secret Service agent took his arm, Trump grabbed the agent’s neck, Hutchinson said. Along with then–White House chief of staff Mark Meadows, she said that Trump wanted his armed supporters to march that day, and overheard a conversation where Trump said that Mike Pence “deserves it,” regarding chants of “hang Mike Pence” heard during the riot.
For now, though, Hutchinson won’t face criminal charges, although MAGA Republicans may try to indict her again. Trump keeps trying to settle old scores in his second term as president. For instance, his Department of Justice just
charged
former FBI Director James Comey for a third time.

Judge Trashes DOJ Attempt to Add More Charges Over Hunter Biden Laptop
Federal prosecutors failed to get judicial approval before filing a new criminal complaint against two former FBI officials who investigated Hunter Biden.
Hunter Biden
Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc/Getty Images
Hunter Biden
A federal judge tore into Department of Justice prosecutors Tuesday for attempting to file a new criminal complaint alleging that two ex-FBI agents had lied to Congress during the investigation into Hunter Biden’s laptop.
At a hearing, Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui refused to rubber-stamp a superseding complaint, which would have added an obstruction charge against former intelligence analyst Nikki Floris, who ran the intelligence division at the Washington field office, and Bradley Benavides, a former deputy assistant director for counterintelligence.
Faruqui told the prosecutors that if they wanted to file a superseding complaint, then a judge would first have to find probable cause that a crime had actually been committed. Instead, the government had transmitted the complaint in the middle of the night and expected the judge to sign it.
Faruqui said that he believed the government had broken precedent by seeking a superseding complaint without first receiving a judge’s signature.
“I’m concerned with your view as to what the law is,” he said. “Why today, on this one case, have you changed the entire history of how you charge cases?”
After Floris and Benavides
surrendered
on arrest warrants last week, Faruqui had ordered a hearing on Tuesday in order to establish probable cause for the initial complaint. Notably, the DOJ
did not obtain
a grand jury indictment against the ex-officials.
On Monday night, the government submitted an unsworn affidavit it claimed would support an additional obstruction charge. Faruqui
refused
to sign the complaint without reviewing it or finding probable cause, and proceeded to hold the hearing on just the first complaint.

Blanche notably did not ask Maxwell any questions about a certain Jane Doe, despite the fact that she was on the FBI’s radar for alleging that Trump attempted to orally rape her and subsequently punched her in the head when she was 14 years old.
At one point in the interview, Blanche
vaguely mentioned testimony

Brazil’s Flávio Bolsonaro Flees to U.S. After Election Defeat
Brazil’s far-right presidential candidate wants to claim political asylum in the United States, sources say.
Presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro looks on after the results of Brazil’s general election were reported on October 25.
Ton Molina/Getty Images
Presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro looks on after the results of Brazil’s general election were reported on October 25.
Fl
á
vio Bolsonaro has fled to the United States after losing Brazil’s presidential election to incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in Brazil on Sunday, according to multiple outlets.
Bolsonaro’s allies reported his escape to
CNN Brasil
, and Reuters also confirmed he is headed to the United States, citing two sources.
Brazilian outlet
O Globo
reported that the far-right senator “was convinced he could be arrested.” While both Bolsonaro’s departure point and destination are uncertain, another Brazilian outlet,
Metropoles
, reported that he was on his way to Florida to seek asylum. Bolsonaro’s father, former President Jair Bolsonaro, is currently serving out house arrest in Brazil for attempting his own version of January 6. He also fled to Florida in late 2022 briefly before his arrest.
Bolsonaro, who has still not conceded the election to Lula, had his passport canceled by the Brazilian Supreme Court due to an active money-laundering case against him. He had until 11 a.m. Tuesday to turn his passport in, but of course didn’t show—fueling questions about his whereabouts.
The Trump administration has been quite friendly to Bolsonaro’s family and antagonistic toward Lula’s left-leaning government.
Neither Bolsonaro nor the U.S. State Department have made a public statement.
