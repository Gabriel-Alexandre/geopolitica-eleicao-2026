What Orsi and His Government Said
Orsi wrote that he had “congratulated him on the victory and on the democratic celebration that an electoral process represents”. The wording is a translation of his Spanish post, as quoted by Subrayado and Montevideo Portal.
Vice President Carolina Cosse called Brazil a “great example” for democracy and Lula’s win “enormous”, Montevideo Portal reported. Subrayado quoted the same post, which opens with “Long live democracy”.
On Friday, 23 October, Orsi said Uruguay must have the best relationship with Brazil whatever the result. Subrayado reported the remark, and the business outlet Ámbito carried it too.
On Wednesday, 14 October, Foreign Minister Mario Lubetkin said ties with the United States should be good but “not conditioned”, Ámbito reported. He added that Uruguay would work with whoever wins once the election results were in.

Why Brazil Matters More Than Washington Here
Uruguay XXI, the state export agency, put September goods exports at US$1,398 million, up 10%. On Saturday, 24 October, Ámbito said Brazil bought US$200 million, 14% of the total and 25% more than a year earlier.
That made Brazil Uruguay’s second-largest buyer, behind China at US$410 million (29%), Ámbito reported. The European Union took US$184 million, the United States US$160 million and neighbouring Argentina US$42 million.
Vehicles and dairy products were each worth about US$31 million of the sales to Brazil, Ámbito said. Brazil took 38% of all Uruguayan dairy exports that month.
Orsi and Lula last met in New York on Monday, 21 September, to discuss farm trade and a Yaguarón River bridge. Lula thanked Uruguay for letting Brazil use the surplus of its beef export quota to China, Montevideo Portal reported.
Opposition Voices Split From the Government
The Frente Amplio’s official account wrote that “Lula won, democracy won”, and Senator Mario Bergara said “democracy won”. Montevideo Portal collected the reactions on Sunday evening.
Opposition Senator Sebastián Da Silva said “the confusion is total”. He argued a minister who had once been Lula’s lawyer, not the electoral court, ordered the extension.
Subrayado reported that Supreme Court judge Cristiano Zanin extended voting by two hours, to 19:00, because voters struggled to reach polling stations. Da Silva’s description of who ordered it is his own claim and is not confirmed by the sources reviewed.
Opposition deputy Felipe Schipani criticised the Frente Amplio for opening its headquarters to follow the results, calling it “a Lula bunker”. Montevideo Portal reported that he shared the remark on X.
Flávio Bolsonaro Refuses to Concede
Flávio Bolsonaro said on Sunday night that “it is impossible to recognise the result” and that he would seek annulment. Montevideo Portal cited his claims of irregularities but no evidence, and no body to receive the request was named.
Brazil’s electoral court, the TSE, counted 64,256,232 votes (51.79%) for Lula and 59,826,041 (48.21%) for Flávio Bolsonaro. That is a margin of about 4.4 million votes.
What It Means for US Readers
Trump’s comment puts him at odds with a neighbouring government that has just congratulated the winner. No Uruguayan response to Trump was found in the sources reviewed.
For exporters and investors, Brazil and China each outranked the United States as Uruguayan buyers in September. One month of data is a snapshot, not a trend.

What Is Not Known
No Uruguayan official was found answering Trump’s remark. Trump’s words come from Montevideo Portal’s Spanish text, so the English wording is a translation.
The export breakdown and Lubetkin’s 14 October remarks come from Ámbito alone; Uruguay XXI’s page confirms only the US$1,398 million total. Whether Bolsonaro will file his request, and where, is unknown.
What Comes Next
Lula’s inauguration is planned for Tuesday, 5 January 2027, Ámbito reported. Bolsonaro has not said which body will receive his annulment request.
Uruguay XXI’s next monthly trade report will show whether Brazil keeps its second place. Uruguay’s government has said it will work with whoever governs Brazil.
