The United States will issue visas to Flávio Bolsonaro and his family to enter the country after he fled Brazil following his election defeat, according to a senior State Department official.
Senator Bolsonaro, 45, was defeated by incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (known as Lula) on Sunday.
While he cried foul and vowed to challenge the election legally, his party and allies accepted the election result, leaving him increasingly isolated.
Senator Bolsonaro's departure from Brazil comes just days after his stunning election loss to incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.
(
Reuters: Suamy Beydoun
)
Senator Bolsonaro last spoke publicly on Monday afternoon, local time, in a social media video, saying he had been banned from leaving the country and ordered to surrender his passports.
However, while he said he would comply with the court order, he had failed to do so by Tuesday afternoon.
Local sources then reported he had fled, possibly via Argentina, to the US.
"The State Department will issue visas to Flávio Bolsonaro and his family and allow them entry into the United States," a senior US State Department official said later.
The official did not confirm whether Senator Bolsonaro had landed in the US.
Senator Bolsonaro is under legal scrutiny in several cases in Brazil, including a corruption probe over the financing of a biopic about his father, Dark Horse , former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is under house arrest for plotting a coup.
The ABC has contacted the US State Department for further comment.
Mystery around Bolsonaro's exit
Earlier, a police source told AFP on condition of anonymity that while Senator Bolsonaro's passports had been cancelled, there was "no formal record of Flávio Bolsonaro leaving the country".
Argentinian newspaper Pagina 12 reported a private jet had flown from Buenos Aires to Puerto Iguazú, the famed tourist town surrounding the Iguazú waterfalls along the Brazil-Argentina border, and was expected to pick up four passengers, including Senator Bolsonaro.
Lula has accused the US of colluding with the Bolsonaros to put them back in power.
(
Reuters: Amanda Perobelli
)
Mauricio Marcon, the deputy leader of Senator Bolsonaro's Liberal Party, also posted to social media claiming that the presidential candidate had left Brazil on a plane.
"Just like María Corina Machado when she fled Nicolás Maduro's dictatorship in Venezuela, Flávio Bolsonaro — the candidate defeated in the election — is also seeking asylum in another country," he said in a post on social media.
AFP observed a motorcade leaving his residence in Brasilia, but could not determine whether the senator was in any of the vehicles.
The US embassy in Brazil did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Senator Bolsonaro's lawyer also did not respond.
Local media reported Senator Bolsonaro may have left Brazil via Argentina en route to the US.
(
AP: Eraldo Peres
)
It was not immediately clear where Senator Bolsonaro was headed, when he would arrive, or whether he would seek asylum.
Argentina's security ministry and migration authorities denied local media reports on Tuesday that Senator Bolsonaro had entered the neighbouring country.
The leadership group of Lula's Workers' Party said in a statement on X that it had filed a preventative detention order for Senator Bolsonaro and requested an INTERPOL red notice.
Red notices ask law enforcement agencies worldwide to locate and detain an individual, pending further extradition or criminal legal action.
Bolsonaro's link to Trump administration
US President Donald Trump's administration has previously alleged Senator Bolsonaro's father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, is a victim of judicial persecution in Brazil.
Senator Bolsonaro has spoken about his relationship with Mr Trump and visited the White House in May.
Lula's celebrates in Brazil as Bolsonaro rejects election results
Two days before his 81st birthday, Brazil's president clinches a historic fourth term. But his opponent is refusing to accept defeat.
In June, his brother Eduardo was convicted in absentia by Brazilian authorities of illegally lobbying the Trump administration and sentenced to four years in prison. Authorities recently issued a warrant for his arrest.
Senator Bolsonaro's departure for the US threatens to deepen a rift between the two most populous nations in the Americas.
Lula has accused the US of colluding with the Bolsonaros to put them back in power, and Reuters reported ahead of Sunday's election that the US State Department is running a campaign to restore them to office.
Lula's office did not respond to a request for comment.

Senator Bolsonaro said the order to surrender his passports stemmed from a comment by Eduardo Bolsonaro, who told a US podcast two days before the election that "if my brother does not win, I am in favour that he would leave the country".
"I never had any intention of leaving the country. I will surrender my passports and defend myself against this travesty," Senator Bolsonaro said.
Trump claims election was rigged
Lula, who turned 81 on Tuesday, staged a dramatic comeback after losing to Senator Bolsonaro in a first-round vote.
After a revamped campaign and
massive mobilisation from the left
, he pulled about 4.4 million more votes than his rival.
Senator Bolsonaro refused to accept the result, complaining about extended voting hours and accusing Lula of abusing power over a flurry of social-spending announcements during the campaign.
While world leaders on both sides of the political divide congratulated Lula, Mr Trump, an ally of the Bolsonaro family, said the election "looked terrible to me".
"I didn't take sides, but it looked like another rigged election frankly," he told reporters in Washington.
"It looked so corrupt."
