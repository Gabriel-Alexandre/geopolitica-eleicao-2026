The Briefing:
Lula da Silva
secured Brazil's presidency for a fourth term on October 25, pulling 51.79% in the runoff against Senator Flávio Bolsonaro — a stunning reversal after finishing second in the October 4 first round.
On election night,
Milei
shared social media posts questioning
Brazil's
extension of voting hours, and in the days surrounding the vote he repeatedly called Lula a "thief" and a "corrupt convict" — insults he had deployed publicly since July.
Sources inside the Casa Rosada told La Nación that recognizing Lula's victory is not on the agenda, the Argentine Chancellery has issued no statement, and Milei remains the only South American leader yet to acknowledge the result.
While governments across the hemisphere lined up to recognize Lula da Silva's fourth presidential term, Argentina's
Javier Milei
spent the days surrounding the Brazilian election calling him a
"corrupt thief"
on social media — and sources inside the Casa Rosada confirmed to La Nación that no official statement of recognition is expected.
Milei's refusal to acknowledge Lula's October 25 runoff victory is not diplomatic awkwardness. It is a calculated posture that has left Argentina isolated among its neighbors, straining ties with its single largest trading partner heading into a high-stakes 2027 election year.
A Comeback Nobody Saw Coming
Lula da Silva pulled off one of the more striking reversals
in Latin American electoral history. After finishing second in Brazil's October 4 first round — trailing Flávio Bolsonaro
47% to 45.1%
, defying pre-election polls that had given him a consistent lead — the incumbent clawed back to win the October 25 runoff by more than 4.4 million votes, taking 51.79% of valid votes against Bolsonaro's 48.21%, according to Brazil's
Superior Electoral Court
. He becomes the first Brazilian leader to hold four presidential terms. Congratulations arrived from across the political spectrum in every country in the hemisphere — except one.

What Milei Did on Election Night
As results came in from across Brazil, Milei did not remain quiet.
He shared social media posts questioning Brazil's decision to extend voting hours
in certain regions — a move the Superior Electoral Court confirmed was a standard administrative response to infrastructure delays in parts of the country. The following morning, he posted against what he called the "average leftie." Neither he nor Foreign Minister Pablo Quirno issued any statement on the election outcome.
The attacks on Lula himself had been building for weeks. Five days before the runoff, Milei wrote on X that he hoped Lula's
"latest dirty campaign trick" would not involve "stealing the election"
— labeling him a "left-wing thief." Brazil's Foreign Ministry called the post an "unacceptable attempt at external interference." On October 7, Milei had posted "LULA LADRÓN" over the Brazilian president's image on Instagram.
Trump echoed similar skepticism. According to
MercoPress
, the U.S. president claimed the result followed "the Biden playbook" and questioned the extended voting hours. Milei's team was watching Washington closely, sources told La Nación.
The Cabinet Meeting Nobody Talked About
The morning after Lula's win, Milei gathered his full cabinet at the Casa Rosada. Brazil went unmentioned. According to multiple sources including
MDZ
, Milei devoted considerable time to attacking what he described as
Lula's "plan platita"
— the social spending push Lula deployed in the final stretch of the campaign — and framing his own economic path as the principled alternative heading into Argentina's 2027 campaign season.
"Changing course to win elections is immoral, unjust, a technical error and a serious affront to the credibility built over these years," Milei reportedly told his ministers, adding: "We will not move a single millimeter from the ideas of freedom." He closed the meeting by framing next year's Argentine vote as a choice "between the party of freedom versus the party of the State and the past."
Three weeks earlier, after Bolsonaro's first-round lead, that same cabinet had reacted with open celebration. La Nación reported Milei called the first-round result "a great victory" and declared that Lula's social spending strategy "hadn't produced the expected result."
A Campaign of Hostility — And the Diplomatic Wreckage
Milei's refusal to recognize the result does not exist in a vacuum. It is the latest chapter in more than a year of personal attacks that have already damaged one of South America's most consequential bilateral relationships. In July, Milei traveled to São Paulo to attend the PL national convention and Flávio Bolsonaro's campaign launch — where he called Lula a "thief," a "convict," and "socialist scum" before an audience.
Brazil recalled its ambassador from Buenos Aires in response
— the first such diplomatic downgrade between the two neighbors in contemporary history. Relations have operated at chargé d'affaires level ever since.
When reporters pressed Milei about the personal nature of the attacks, he was unapologetic: "I didn't attack the Brazilian people," he said in a radio interview. "I spoke about Lula, the corrupt one."
Isolated Among Allies
What makes Milei's posture most striking,
as the Buenos Aires Herald noted
, is that it placed him to the right of every other right-wing leader in the hemisphere. Chile's José Antonio Kast told reporters he had spoken directly with Lula by phone to offer congratulations. Paraguay's Santiago Peña, Bolivia's Rodrigo Paz, Peru's Keiko Fujimori and
Colombia's Abelardo de la Espriella
all acknowledged the result. Even Mauricio Macri's PRO party — an official coalition partner of Milei's La Libertad Avanza — was reported by La Nación to have congratulated Lula independently, calling the moment "a new opportunity to strengthen a bond that is historic and permanent."
The waiting game inside the Casa Rosada has a documented rationale. Sources cited by
MDZ
reported that an internal directive told officials to withhold comment until it became clear whether
Flávio Bolsonaro would pursue a formal legal challenge
to the results. For now, Milei is effectively holding out to see whether the result itself holds before deciding his next move.
The Cost of a Personal Grudge
The commercial stakes are stark:
Brazil is Argentina's largest export market
, and the two nations are Mercosur's dominant economies. A Zuban Córdoba survey conducted in August 2026 had already found that 70.5% of Argentines disapproved of Milei's attacks on Lula — with nearly nine in ten viewing the economic relationship with Brazil as critical. Argentina now faces the next two years of Lula in power with relations at a historic low, no resident ambassador in Buenos Aires, and a president next door who has every reason to return the hostility.
