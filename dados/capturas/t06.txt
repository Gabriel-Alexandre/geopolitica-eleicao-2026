What Trump Said About Lula’s Win
EFE’s Spanish text, carried by Infobae with a Washington dateline, quotes Trump saying Brazil had “just applied” Biden’s playbook. None of the outlets says where he spoke, or whether he spoke or posted.
He added that Bolsonaro had a big lead in the first round and was the clear favourite in the runoff. He also said “communists” do not give up power once they win it.
Times Brasil, Cadena 3 of Argentina and Diário do País run the same words and credit the agency. All give a Spanish or Portuguese text, so the English wording here is a translation.
EFE says Trump compared the result to his own 2020 defeat by Biden, which he refused to accept. None of the outlets reports evidence of irregularities from Trump or his aides.

How the Runoff Count Ended
The Superior Electoral Court (TSE) count gives Lula 64,256,232 votes (51.79%) and Flávio Bolsonaro 59,826,041 (48.21%), with all sections totalled. That is a margin of about 4.4 million votes among more than 124 million valid votes.
In the first round on Sunday, 4 October, Flávio led with 47.03% to Lula’s 45.16%, Portal de Prefeitura reported from TSE data. That was a lead of about 1.9 points, or 2.2 million votes, not the wide gap Trump described.
Polls before the runoff had pointed to a near tie, with Flávio slightly ahead, Cadena 3 reported. Lula will be sworn in on 5 January 2027, the same outlet says.
The Voting Extension Behind the Dispute
Supreme Federal Court (STF) Justice Cristiano Zanin extended voting on Sunday after complaints of public-transport shortages, mainly in Salvador and São Paulo. Flávio blamed that decision for a defeat he said he could not recognise.
The TSE says the extension ran until 19:00 Brasília time and applied only where polls were still open. Mix Vale reports the same, and that publication of results was delayed.
Flávio said the election should have been annulled and that recognising the result was “impossible,” Todapalavra reported. Diário do País adds that Martin De Luca, a lawyer for Trump Media and Rumble, criticised the ruling on X.

Washington’s Position Before the Vote
On 15 October the State Department replied to a 24 September letter from Senator Bernie Sanders and 30 House Democrats. Katherine Bowles of its legislative affairs office signed the reply, which Folha de S.Paulo obtained, Jornal de Brasília reported.
The reply says the United States supports the Brazilian people’s right to a free and fair electoral process. It did not commit to respecting the outcome, which the lawmakers had asked for, Sociedade Militar reported.
Sanders congratulated Lula on Sunday night, while Trump had said nothing about the result, ICL Notícias reported earlier that evening. Chile’s President José Antonio Kast and Paraguay’s Santiago Peña also sent congratulations, ICL Notícias and Times Brasil reported.
Trade is also a pressure point, as the US has charged a 25% tariff on Brazilian imports since Wednesday, 22 July 2026. A US Trade Representative notice in the Federal Register of 20 July lists certain exemptions.
What It Means for US Readers
Investors in Brazilian stocks, bonds and US-listed Brazil funds should note that the remark is political, not legal. It does not change the count, but it keeps US-Brazil relations in the headlines.
Exporters and importers still face the 25% tariff, and nothing found shows a change after the vote. Any shift would come from the White House or USTR, not from the Brazilian courts.

What Is Not Known
The outlets do not say whether Trump spoke to reporters, posted online or issued a statement, or at what time. The original English words were not found, so the wording here rests on EFE’s translation.
No White House or State Department statement recognising or disputing the result was found by Monday, 26 October. It is also unclear whether Flávio has filed his annulment request, and the TSE’s formal certification date is not confirmed.
Earlier partial counts differed slightly, for example 51.78% at 99.95% of sections in Mix Vale. The final TSE count rounds to 51.79%.
What Comes Next
Watch for a formal US statement on Lula’s win and for any filing by Flávio’s party with the electoral court. Neither step would by itself change the result, which Brazil’s courts decide.
Lula’s inauguration is set for Tuesday, 5 January 2027, in Brasília. Whether Trump or a US delegation attends is not known.

What was the result of Brazil’s runoff?

Has Flávio Bolsonaro conceded?

Has the US recognised Lula’s victory?
