Brazilian Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro fled Brazil for the
United States
on Tuesday, two days after losing the country's presidential runoff to leftist incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.
The U.S.
Department of State
will issue visas to Bolsonaro and his family and allow them to enter the U.S., a senior State Department official confirmed to Fox News Digital.
An ally of the far-right conservative senator confirmed his departure from the South American country to Reuters on Tuesday.
Bolsonaro's flight was reportedly headed to Florida, according to Brazilian outlet
Metropoles
, citing people close to his family.

The 45-year-old
lawmaker had
been out of public view since Tuesday morning, the outlet reported.
His departure came as a deadline for him to surrender his regular and
diplomatic passports
expired at noon Tuesday.
Brazilian newspaper Folha de São Paulo reported Bolsonaro was under investigation for alleged money laundering and other crimes.
It remained unclear whether Bolsonaro had complied with the order, The Associated Press reported.

Bolsonaro was served with the order Monday, a day after Lula defeated him in
Sunday's presidential runoff
for an unprecedented fourth term.
With 100% of the vote counted, Lula won 51.8% of the vote, defeating Bolsonaro by nearly 4.4 million votes.
He has refused to concede the election and has called for the election results to be annulled, alleging fraud, according to The Associated Press.
Lula addressed supporters after winning re-election, expressing gratitude to the
Brazilian people
.
